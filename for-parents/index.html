For parents

The rest of this site talks to explorers. This page talks to you.

Plain, specific and unglamorous. If something here is not clear enough to act on, it is not written well enough, and we would like to know.

Who is with them

Three named adults, and what each one is for.

Lead

Michael Daymond

Lead Safari & Wildlife Expert and Chairperson of Dinokeng Game Enterprises. Runs the field programme and makes the call on wildlife proximity and route.

Safety

Designated Safety Officer

A dedicated role with Dinokeng operating experience, not a guide doing two jobs. Enforces reserve protocol and holds the Emergency Response Plan.

Pastoral

Female chaperone

Present at all times so there is male and female supervision throughout, including overnight. First point of contact for anything pastoral.

Your child is never alone in a free-roaming Big Five environment, and movement between buildings after dark is escorted. The full hour-by-hour shape of a day is on the expedition page.

If something goes wrong

What actually happens.

Homesick

If your child is homesick

It is common, it usually peaks on nights two and three, and it usually passes. The chaperone will notice it before your child raises it.

The first response is practical: an early call home outside the usual window, a job to do, and being paired with someone in the group. We will tell you it is happening rather than manage it silently, and we will tell you honestly whether it is settling.

If it does not settle, we will talk to you about options rather than leaving a miserable teenager to get through it alone.

Unwell

If your child is unwell

Minor illness is handled on site, and you are told the same day, not at the end of the week.

Anything that needs a doctor means a trip to a clinic with a supervising adult, with you informed before we go, not after. Anything that needs more than a clinic means Netcare Montana Hospital in Pretoria, which runs a 24-hour emergency department and trauma service.

Your child is never taken to a medical facility unaccompanied.

Injury

If your child is injured

All supervising adults carry first-aid capability and the group carries a stocked kit on every drive and walk.

For anything beyond a graze, the Safety Officer takes over, the Emergency Response Plan is activated, and you are called. Evacuation is by road ambulance, or by aeromedical evacuation where it is clinically necessary and available.

Emergency

The emergency escalation chain, in order

1. The incident is stabilised on site by the nearest supervising adult, and the Safety Officer is called immediately.

2. The Safety Officer assesses and decides: manage on site, clinic, or full emergency response. That decision belongs to one named person, not a committee.

3. Emergency services and, where required, aeromedical evacuation are activated. Dinokeng’s own reserve response structure is available in parallel.

4. You are called by a founder, by phone, as soon as your child is stable and safe. Not by message, and not by someone junior.

5. Your insurer and the receiving hospital are given the medical documentation you supplied at booking.

6. You receive continuous updates until it is resolved, and a written account afterwards.

A parent from every family is reachable by phone for the duration of the expedition. That is a condition of travel, not a preference.

Group

If your child does not get on with the group

Eight people, eight nights. It occasionally happens.

The group is deliberately small and the staff ratio deliberately high precisely so that a supervising adult can notice this on day two rather than day six. Room pairings can be changed. Activity pairings are rotated by design.

We do not pretend every group becomes lifelong friends. We do make sure nobody spends the week on the edge of it.

Rooming and supervision

Where they sleep.

A fully fenced, secure lodge inside the reserve. Two students per room, each in their own single bed, with a private en-suite bathroom.

Male and female students are accommodated separately, with rooming planned in advance rather than sorted out on arrival. Supervising adults sleep in the same lodge environment and are available throughout the night.

The one exception is the Day 06 sleep-out on a managed open platform, under continuous adult supervision. If you would prefer your child not to take part, they can opt out with no effect on the rest of the programme, and no fuss made about it.

Devices

The phone policy, stated plainly.

Phones come along. They are not confiscated and they are not banned.

They are put away during game drives, walks, field sessions and meals, for the obvious reason that you cannot track an animal through a screen and a ringtone carries a long way in the bush.

There is a scheduled evening window for calls and messages home. Reserve connectivity is genuinely patchy in places, which is a fact of the location rather than a policy.

If your family would prefer a stricter arrangement for your own child, tell us at application and we will hold to it.

What you pay and when

In one paragraph.

US$3,950 per explorer for the founding December 2026 cohort. A US$750 non-refundable deposit holds the place; the balance is due 60 days before departure. Cancel more than 60 days out and you lose the deposit only. Between 60 and 30 days, half the balance is refundable. Inside 30 days there is no refund, which is exactly what your travel insurance is for, and why we require it.

And the one that matters most: if fewer than four explorers have booked 60 days before departure, we cancel and refund everything, deposit included.
