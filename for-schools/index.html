IB

IB Diploma — Creativity, Activity, Service

The expedition offers material against all three CAS strands: activity through sustained physical fieldwork, service through habitat restoration and survey work that feeds real reserve management, and creativity through the Conservation Passport and the closing presentation.

It also supports a CAS project rather than only isolated experiences, because the work is continuous across eight days and produces an output.

Hour counts and evidence requirements are set by your school’s CAS coordinator against IB guidance. We supply a written record of activity, dates and supervision to support a student’s reflection, and we do not tell you what it is worth.

DofE

The Duke of Edinburgh’s International Award

The programme is structured to support the Residential Project section at Gold level, being a purposeful shared activity with people the participant does not know, over the required consecutive period.

Elements of the expedition also map to the Adventurous Journey section, though whether a given itinerary satisfies your Award Unit’s requirements is a decision for your Award Leader and assessor, not for us.

We provide supervision records and an activity log in whatever format your Award Unit needs.

IGCSE / A-level

Geography and biology fieldwork

Geography: ecosystems and biomes, conservation and management of the natural environment, sustainable resource use, and the human geography of land use conflict and community livelihoods.

Biology: biodiversity and classification, sampling and survey technique, population monitoring, adaptation, food chains and trophic structure, and human impact on ecosystems.

Students collect and record real data using the methods reserve staff use, which is materially different from observing a demonstration.
