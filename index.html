The mission

Conservation is not something you learn about. It’s something you do.

Most teenagers meet African wildlife through a screen. The distance is the problem. A documentary can tell a fifteen-year-old that a species is in trouble; it cannot put them on the ground beside the people whose job it is to do something about it.

Dinokeng is a malaria-free Big Five reserve an hour from Johannesburg. For eight nights, a group of no more than eight explorers works inside it — tracking, monitoring, recording, restoring — alongside guides and conservation staff who do this work when no one is watching.

The learning is experiential because that is the only kind that survives the flight home.

The vision

A generation that feels responsible for what it protects.

An informed advocate is worth more to conservation than a hundred passive admirers. The difference between the two is usually contact: having stood in a landscape, understood what threatens it, and met the people carrying the cost of protecting it.

We are not trying to produce conservationists. We are trying to produce adults who, whatever they end up doing, cannot un-know what they saw.

How the programme works

Three pillars. One expedition.

Pillar 01 — Learn

Understand the problem properly.

Biodiversity, endangered species, habitat loss and wildlife crime, taught without simplification. Every explorer receives a Conservation Passport to record what they learn in the field.

Pillar 02 — Experience

Get into the landscape.

Game drives, bush walks, tracking, cheetah feeding, camera-trap work, and one night sleeping out under the stars on an open platform.

Pillar 03 — Contribute

Leave something behind.

Real conservation work: habitat restoration, biodiversity surveys and data collection that feeds into how the reserve is managed.

The eight days

What actually happens, day by day.

Day 01

Arrival & welcome

Reception at OR Tambo, transfer to the reserve, safety briefing, Conservation Passports issued.

Day 02

The conservation landscape

Ecosystem introduction, first bush walk covering tracking basics, sundowner game drive.

Day 03

Conservation in the field

Wildlife monitoring, camera-trap demonstrations, habitat restoration work.

Day 04

Wildlife & biodiversity

Full-day drives at Mongena tracking the Big Five, bird and plant identification, evening talk.

Day 05

Predators & awareness

Kevin Richardson Wildlife Sanctuary, bush-skills session, night drive for nocturnal species.

Day 06

Sleeping under the stars

Radio-telemetry demonstration, then a night on an open sleep-out platform. No tents, no walls.

Day 07

People, culture & conservation

Human-wildlife coexistence, community cultural exchange, leadership workshop.

Days 08–09

Legacy day & departure

Passport reflections, explorer presentations, certificate ceremony, transfer to OR Tambo.
