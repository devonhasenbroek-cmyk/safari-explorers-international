Safety & supervision

Is Dinokeng safe? What about crime in South Africa?

South Africa has a serious crime problem and pretending otherwise would insult your intelligence. It is also a country the size of Western Europe, and the risk profile of a fenced private game reserve an hour from Johannesburg is not the risk profile of a city centre at night.

Explorers are inside a controlled reserve environment for essentially the whole expedition. The transfer from OR Tambo is private, direct and accompanied. Nobody is on public transport, nobody is walking through a town, and nobody is unsupervised outside the lodge.

The genuine hazards here are wildlife, heat and vehicles, in that order, and those are what our procedures are actually built around.

How many adults are with the group?

Three, for a maximum of eight explorers: Michael Daymond as Lead Safari and Wildlife Expert, a designated Safety Officer, and a female chaperone. That is a ratio better than one adult to every three children.

There is male and female supervision at every point, including overnight, and supervising adults sleep in the same lodge.

Is my child ever alone with a single adult?

Activities are run in groups, and the structure is designed so that one-to-one situations do not arise as a matter of routine.

Every adult working with students has been through identity verification, criminal background screening and safeguarding checks before the programme starts.

The sleep-out night sounds risky. Is it?

It is a managed platform inside the reserve with continuous adult supervision through the night, not a group of teenagers left in the open.

It is also completely optional. If you would rather your child slept at the lodge that night, they can, and no fuss will be made about it in front of the group.

How close do you get to dangerous animals?

Close enough to see properly, from a vehicle, under the direction of a guide with 18 years of experience in this reserve and a Safety Officer whose entire job is that judgement call.

Bush walks happen in open terrain chosen for visibility, in single file, with strict discipline about noise and movement. Explorers are briefed on day one and the briefing is not a formality.

Health & medical

Is the reserve malaria-free?

Dinokeng is in a malaria-free area, which is one of the main reasons the programme is based here rather than further north. No antimalarial prophylaxis is required.

You should still confirm current health advice for South Africa with your own doctor or travel clinic before departure.

What vaccinations does my child need?

That is a question for your doctor or a travel clinic, not for us, and any operator who gives you a definitive list on a web page is overstepping.

Requirements depend on your child’s medical history, existing vaccination record and the country they are travelling from. Start that conversation early, because some schedules need weeks.

What if my child takes regular medication?

Tell us at application. All medication travels clearly labelled and with written administration instructions, and requirements are reviewed and communicated to supervising adults before the programme starts.

Emergency medication such as inhalers or adrenaline auto-injectors stays immediately accessible at all times, including on drives and walks.

My child has a severe allergy. Can they still come?

In most cases yes, with an individual management plan agreed in writing before travel.

Tell us the full picture at application. If we judge that a condition cannot be safely managed in a remote environment an hour from a hospital, we will say so before you pay a deposit rather than after. That is a small number of cases, but we would rather disappoint you early than take a risk with your child.

Travel & visas

Does my child fly alone?

Flights are booked by families, and most explorers will fly on the direct service from their home city. All three launch markets have one.

Airlines operate Unaccompanied Minor services with their own age bands, fees and rules, which must be confirmed with the carrier directly. Where enough explorers depart from the same city we will try to coordinate a group flight or a flight-buddy pairing, though neither is confirmed until the cohort is full.

What documents does South Africa require for a child?

South Africa applies additional documentation requirements to travellers under 18, and they are stricter than most countries. Requirements have historically involved additional proof of parental relationship and documented consent where a child is not travelling with both parents.

Verify the current requirements directly with your nearest South African mission and the Department of Home Affairs, then confirm with your airline, because the airline decides whether your child boards. Do not rely on any operator’s website, including this one.

Once your place is confirmed we will help you assemble the documentation and check it before you travel.

Do you book the flights?

No. Flights and travel insurance are arranged by parents or guardians, and are not included in the programme fee.

We will advise on routing and timing so your child arrives inside the transfer window, and we will tell you what your insurance needs to cover.

What if the flight is delayed or cancelled?

Tell us as soon as you know and we will adjust the airport collection. Someone will be there whenever your child actually lands.

If a delay costs your child part of the programme, that is a travel insurance matter rather than a refund matter, which is one of several reasons cover is mandatory.

Money & booking

What exactly does the fee cover?

Everything on the ground: eight nights’ accommodation, all meals and drinking water, all in-reserve transport, every game drive and activity, full-time qualified guides and supervision, airport transfers both ways, and the Conservation Passport and programme materials.

It does not cover international flights, travel and medical insurance, visa fees, or personal spending money.

What if you do not get enough bookings?

Then we cancel and refund you in full, including the non-refundable deposit. The expedition runs with a minimum of four explorers, and if we have not reached four by 60 days before departure you will be told immediately.

You will never be left holding a flight booking for an expedition that is not happening.

Can I pay in instalments?

The standard structure is a US$750 deposit to hold the place and the balance 60 days before departure.

If a different schedule would make this possible for your family, ask. We would rather have the conversation than lose a good candidate over cash flow.

The December dates run over Christmas.

They do, and we say so plainly on the dates page rather than hiding it.

Those dates are the only window that falls inside the year-end break in Singapore, the UAE and Australia simultaneously, which is what makes a genuinely international group possible. If it does not work for your family, the July 2027 window exists for exactly that reason.

Daily life on the expedition

Can my child call home?

Yes. There is a scheduled phone window built into the evening routine, and times are published to families before departure so nobody is waiting up.

Phones are put away during drives, walks, field sessions and meals. Reserve connectivity is genuinely patchy in places, which is geography rather than policy.

What is the food like, and can you handle dietary requirements?

Three cooked meals a day plus snacks and drinking water, catered at the lodge.

Vegetarian, halal, allergy-related and other requirements are handled routinely when they are declared at application. Tell us early and it is a non-issue; tell us on arrival and it is harder.

My child has never been away from home this long.

That is one of the more common reasons parents hesitate, and honestly it is one of the better reasons to come.

Homesickness usually peaks on nights two and three and usually passes. The chaperone will normally spot it before your child raises it, and the response is practical: an early call home, a job to do, and being paired up within the group. You will be told it is happening rather than finding out afterwards.

Eight nights is deliberately long enough to matter and short enough to be manageable.

What if my child does not get on with the group?

Eight people over eight nights, so it occasionally happens.

The group is small and the staff ratio high precisely so an adult notices on day two rather than day six. Room pairings can be changed and activity pairings rotate by design. We will not claim every group becomes lifelong friends, but nobody spends the week on the edge of it.

What is the phone and device policy?

Phones come, and they are neither confiscated nor banned. They are put away during drives, walks, field sessions and meals, with a scheduled window in the evening for calls and messages.

If your family wants a stricter arrangement for your own child, tell us at application and we will hold to it.

About the programme

You are a new company with no reviews. Why should I trust you?

You should be asking this, and we would think less of a parent who did not.

What we cannot offer is a review history, and we will not manufacture one. What we can offer is verifiable: the man leading the field programme is the Chairperson of Dinokeng Game Enterprises with 18 years in this reserve. Three of the four founders have spent two decades in education. Every adult is background-screened. The medical facility is named. The insurance is real. And the minimum numbers guarantee means that if this does not come together, you get every cent back.

The founding cohort price reflects the fact that you are taking us on trust. That is the honest trade, and it is stated on the pricing page rather than buried.

Is this actually conservation, or a holiday with a conservation label on it?

Eight teenagers will not save a species in eight nights, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

What eight nights genuinely does is contribute a small amount of real work — monitoring data, camera-trap captures, habitat restoration — that feeds into how this reserve is managed, and does it alongside the people who do it year-round. The data is real, the restoration is real, and neither is staged for visitors.

The larger honest claim is about the explorer rather than the reserve. Eight nights can permanently change what a person notices for the rest of their life, and a generation that notices is worth considerably more to conservation than eight days of labour.

Will my child definitely see the Big Five?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. Dinokeng is a free-roaming Big Five reserve and sightings are genuinely likely across eight days, particularly with the full day at Mongena. They are not guaranteed by anybody honest.

Tracking is the skill being taught. Finding nothing, and understanding why you found nothing, is a real part of the education.

What is the age range, and are the groups mixed?

Thirteen to seventeen, grouped by cohort so the age spread within a single group stays sensible rather than putting a thirteen-year-old with a group of seventeen-year-olds.

Groups are mixed by gender and by nationality, with accommodation arranged separately for male and female students. The international mix is a deliberate part of the design.

What happens after the expedition?

Each explorer leaves with their completed Conservation Passport, a certificate, and the action plan they wrote themselves on Legacy Day.

We will also ask your family, some weeks later, for an honest account of how it went. If it earned it, we will ask permission to quote you. If it did not, we would rather know that than not.
